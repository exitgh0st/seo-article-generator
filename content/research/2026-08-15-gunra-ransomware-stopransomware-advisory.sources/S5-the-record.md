# FBI, South Korea warn of Gunra ransomware gang targeting critical infrastructure

## Agency Warnings

The FBI and South Korea's National Police Agency issued a joint cybersecurity advisory on
August 10, 2026, highlighting the Gunra ransomware operation that emerged in April 2025.

Chris Butera, acting executive assistant director for cybersecurity at CISA, characterized
the threat, stating: "Gunra is another variant in the ongoing trend of ransomware attacks
causing disruption and harm to U.S. and international organizations."

## Technical Details

The gang exploits two Fortinet firewall vulnerabilities:
- CVE-2024-55591
- CVE-2025-24472

These exploits grant privileged access for data theft and encryption before extortion.

## Victim Information

The advisory examined multiple incidents but did not name specific victims. However, it
noted:
- Sectors targeted: Healthcare, financial services, government
- Geographic scope: Global operations
- Ransom demands: Typically exceeded $10 million with 5-7 day payment deadlines

The FBI observed: "Gunra actors attempting to communicate directly with management staff at
victim companies via email to solicit ransom payments with limited success."

## Evolution and Expansion

By January 2026, Gunra transitioned to a ransomware-as-a-service (RaaS) model, recruiting
initial access brokers and using aliases including "Golden Community." The group developed
a Linux variant alongside its original Windows version.

## Incident Metrics

Dragos identified 1,140 ransomware incidents affecting industrial organizations in Q2
2026, a 12% increase from Q1, with at least four attributed to Gunra (compared to eight in
Q1).
